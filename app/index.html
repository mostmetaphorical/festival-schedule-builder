Making the file

The easy way is a spreadsheet. Start from the template: one row per screening, with title, date and time required. Save it as CSV. A film's details only need filling in on one of its rows, and lists like cast go in one cell separated by semicolons.

  1. A screening's title must match the same film's other rows exactly.
  2. Dates like 2027-03-04; times like 7:30 PM or 19:30.
  3. Put event in the kind column for parties and secret screenings — listed, not rated.
  4. The more director, cast, genre and keywords you fill in, the better the recommendations.
  5. For posters, put the image's full https:// link in the poster column — the festival's own listing is usually the best source.

Prefer JSON? This is the whole format, and the repository has scripts that build it from a festival's published schedule. 

{
  "festival": "Some Fest 2027",
  "days": ["2027-03-04", "2027-03-05"],
  "films": [{
    "title": "A Film",
    "year": 2027,
    "runtime": 94,
    "synopsis": "One or two sentences.",
    "poster": "https://…/a-film.jpg",
    "kind": "film",
    "entities": {
      "director": ["A Director"],
      "cast": ["An Actor"],
      "keyword": ["revenge"],
      "genre": ["horror"]
    }
  }],
  "screenings": [{
    "film": "A Film",
    "date": "2027-03-04",
    "time": "7:30 PM"
  }]
}

Help improve the recommendations

Optional

Sharing your ratings lets the recommender be tested against real Letterboxd histories instead of an old research dataset. That's how it gets better.

What's sent
Title, year and star rating for each film.
What isn't
Watch dates, username, reviews, diary, watchlist — anything that identifies you or your device.
Where it goes
A private store only the developer can read. Never published.
How long
Until it's downloaded for testing, then deleted from the store.

Because nothing identifying is kept, a shared file can't be traced back to you — which also means it can't be found later to delete on request. If that matters to you, don't share.

A quick bot check appears after you tick the box

Your ratings and plan are kept in this browser only, so you don't have to import them again. Nothing is sent anywhere and there's no account. Browsers do clear this sometimes — the saved web page is the reliable backup.

Help make the recommendations better

This is an alpha. The model behind it was trained on an old public dataset of mostly mainstream films, and festival slates are mostly premieres nobody has rated yet. More real rating histories make it measurably better, and there's a test in the repository that scores how well it predicts ratings it has never seen.

The easy way: build a plan, then use Share my ratings on the Plan tab. It sends only titles, years and star ratings, and nothing goes anywhere until you tick the box and press the button.

Please only share if you've rated at least 30 films.

Fewer than that is too little for the test to learn anything from.

Emailing instead: what to send, and what not to
  1. On a computer, sign in and open letterboxd.com/settings/data.
  2. Click Export your data. A .zip downloads.
  3. Open the .zip and take out ratings.csv only.
  4. Email that one file to the address shown here. Please don't send the whole .zip.

ratings.csv holds only the date, film title, film year, a link to the film, and your star rating. Your username, real name, email, location, bio, reviews, diary and watchlist are in other files in that zip, which is exactly why only this one is wanted. If you'd rather not share when you watched things, delete the Date column first — it isn't used.
