Making the file

The repository has scripts that build one from a festival's published schedule, and enrich_festival.py fills in credits and posters from TMDB. If you'd rather assemble it by hand, this is the whole format — everything else is optional: 

{
  "festival": "Some Fest 2027",
  "days": ["2027-03-04", "2027-03-05"],
  "films": [
    {
      "title": "A Film",
      "year": 2027,
      "runtime": 94,
      "synopsis": "One or two sentences.",
      "section": "Main Competition",
      "kind": "film",
      "entities": {
        "director": ["A Director"],
        "cast": ["An Actor"],
        "keyword": ["revenge", "small town"],
        "genre": ["horror"]
      }
    }
  ],
  "screenings": [
    { "film": "A Film", "date": "2027-03-04", "time": "7:30 PM", "runtime": 94 }
  ]
}

Two things matter: a screening's film has to match a lineup title exactly, and dates are YYYY-MM-DD. Use "kind": "event" for parties, secret screenings and live shows — those get listed but not rated. The more of entities you fill in, the better the recommendations; with none, every film scores the same.

Help improve the recommendations

Optional. Sharing your ratings lets the recommender be tested against real Letterboxd histories instead of an old research dataset, which is how it gets better.

What's sent
Title, year and star rating for each film.
What isn't
The dates you watched, your username, reviews, diary, watchlist, or anything identifying you or your device.
Where it goes
A private store only the developer can read. Never published or shown to anyone else.
How long
Until it's downloaded for testing, then deleted from the store.

Because nothing identifying is kept, a shared file can't be traced back to you — which also means it can't be found again later to delete on request. If that matters to you, don't share.

Stores your ratings and plan in this browser only, so you don't have to import them again. Nothing is sent anywhere and there's no account. Browsers do clear this sometimes — the downloaded file is the reliable backup.

Help make the recommendations better

This is an alpha. The model behind it was trained on an old public dataset of mostly mainstream films, and it's being tested against real film-festival slates — where most titles are premieres nobody has rated yet. More real rating histories make it measurably better, and there's an honest test in the repository that scores how well it predicts ratings it has never seen.

Please only send yours if you've rated at least 30 films. This isn't gatekeeping — it's that smaller histories can't answer the question being asked. The test hides 30% of someone's ratings and checks whether the recommender predicts them. With 20 ratings that means learning from 14 films and being graded on 6, which is mostly luck: measured across hundreds of people, profiles under 30 scored barely better than assuming someone likes everything equally. Sending a small one means handing over your viewing history for no useful result, which isn't a fair trade. Rate more films and come back — it stays useful.

Emailing instead: what to send, and what not to
  1. On a computer, sign in and open letterboxd.com/settings/data.
  2. Click Export your data. A .zip downloads.
  3. Open the .zip and take out ratings.csv only.
  4. Send that one file. Please don't send the whole .zip.

ratings.csv holds only the date, film title, film year, a link to the film, and your star rating. Your username, real name, email, location, bio, reviews, diary and watchlist are in other files in that zip, which is exactly why only this one is wanted. If you'd rather not share when you watched things, delete the Date column first — it isn't used. And again: 30 ratings or more, please. Below that your history can't answer the question the test asks, so sending it costs you privacy for nothing.

Ratings stay on your device unless you choose to share them. Film details come from TMDB — this product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.
